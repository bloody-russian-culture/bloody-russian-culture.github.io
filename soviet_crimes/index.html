Bloody Soviet Union

The Soviet Union continued Russian Empire’s conquest and terror on an industrial scale. Mass murders, famines, and deportations were conducted against Ukrainians, Crimean Tatars, Latvians, Lithuanians, Estonians, Finns, Poles, Belarussians, Georgians, Kazakhs, Jews, Armenians, Greeks, Germans, Hungarians, Mongolians, Kalmyks, Chechens and Ingushes, Afghans, Balkars, Meskhetian Turks, Koreans, and Japanese.

Opposition to the Russian Orthodox Church during the Russian Civil War 11.1917

During the Russian Civil War, the Bolsheviks killed at least 1 metropolitan, 18 bishops, 102 priests, 154 deacons, and 94 monks and nuns between June 1918 and January 1919. They also attacked and desecrated numerous monasteries and churches.

Makhnovshchina 1918

The Makhnovshchina, an anarchist-communist movement, faced severe repression from the Bolsheviks, resulting in the death of 200,000 Ukrainian peasants.

Pogroms during the Russian Civil War 1918

From 1918 to 1920, over 1,300 pogroms occurred in Ukraine, resulting in between 35,000 and 250,000 deaths. 50,000 to 300,000 children were orphaned.

Basmachi movement 02.1918

The Basmachi movement, an uprising against Russian and Soviet rule in Central Asia, led to the Red Army carrying out a massacre of up to 25,000 people in Kokand in 1918.

Red Terror 05.09.1918

The Red Terror, initiated by the Bolsheviks in September 1918, aimed to suppress political dissent and opposition during the Russian Civil War. Between 1918 and 1922, the campaign led to the execution of at least 50,000 individuals, marking the beginning of the Gulag system and causing thousands more deaths due to harsh conditions in labor camps.

Executed Renaissance 1920

The Executed Renaissance refers to the elimination of Ukrainian artists and intellectuals during the 1920s and early 1930s, with 45 intellectuals convicted, and over 30,000 arrested, deported, or executed following Stalin's rise and the Great Terror of 1937-38.

Tambov Rebellion 19.08.1920

The Tambov Rebellion, from 1920-1922, was a significant peasant uprising against forced grain requisitions by the Bolshevik government, leading to brutal suppression that killed around 15,000 and arrested 100,000 people.

Russian famine of 1921–1922 1921

The Russian famine of 1921-1922, affecting the Volga and Ural regions, killed an estimated 5,000,000 people due to drought, war, economic policies, and disease outbreaks. The Bolshevik government's requisitioning of supplies from peasants without adequate compensation worsened the crisis.

USSR anti-religious campaign (1921–1928) 1921

The USSR anti-religious campaign (1921-1928) aimed to eliminate religion through propaganda and persecution. Approximately 8,100 clergy, monks, and nuns were murdered, churches and monasteries were systematically closed, and the Orthodox Church lost its ability to train new clergy by 1928.

Soviet invasion of Georgia 11.02.1921

The Soviet invasion of Georgia in February 1921, led by Stalin and Ordzhonikidze, culminated in the execution of 12,578 Georgians and the exile of over 20,000 to Siberia in 1924.

Treaty of Riga 18.03.1921

The Treaty of Riga ended the Polish-Soviet War, incorporating 4,000,000 Ukrainians and over 1,000,000 Belarusians into Poland, leading to ethnic tensions. Ethnic Poles within Soviet borders faced severe repression, with at least 111,000 executed in 1937/38.

USSR anti-religious campaign (1928–1941) 1928

Between 1928 and 1941, the Soviet Union enforced an anti-religious campaign that led to the closure of nearly all churches and the imprisonment and death of up to 200,000 clergy, severely curtailing religious practice and expression.

Shakhty Trial 18.05.1928

The Shakhty Trial, with 11 of 53 accused engineers sentenced to death, replacing skilled professionals with politically aligned youth.

Genocide of the Ingrian Finns 1929

The Soviet Union's actions against the Ingrian Finns, including deportations, imprisonments, and killings, resulted in the population declining from 140,000-160,000 to approximately 19,000 by today. Over 100,000 Russian Finns were deported without trial, and tens of thousands died in labor camps.

Dekulakization 1930

Dekulakization, a campaign against so-called kulaks in the Soviet Union, led to mass arrests, executions, and deportations, with estimates suggesting between 530,000 and 600,000 deaths from 1930 to 1933.

Kazakh famine of 1930–1933 1930

The Kazakh famine of 1930-1933, resulting from Soviet collectivization policies, led to the deaths of an estimated 1,300,000 to 2,300,000 people, with 38-42% of all Kazakhs perishing.

Holodomor 1932

The Holodomor, a man-made famine in Ukraine from 1932 to 1933, resulted in between 3,500,000 and 5,000,000 deaths due to Stalin's policies of high grain quotas and low food rations.

Great Purge 08.1936

The Great Purge, conducted from 1936 to 1938, involved the execution or imprisonment of hundreds of thousands of Soviet citizens suspected of political dissent. Estimates suggest between 700,000 to 1,200,000 people were killed.

Vinnytsia massacre 1937

Over 9,400 people were secretly executed in Vinnytsia by the Soviet NKVD in 1937-1938.

NKVD Order No. 00439 25.07.1937

NKVD Order № 00439, enacted in 1937, led to the arrest of around 800 German citizens and former citizens, and sentenced approximately 55,005 Soviet citizens of German descent, with 41,898 receiving death sentences.

NKVD Order No. 00447 30.07.1937

NKVD Order No. 00447 led to the execution of 386,798 Soviet citizens by 1938.

NKVD Order No. 00485 11.08.1937

NKVD Order No. 00485, issued in 1937, was an anti-Polish ethnic cleansing campaign targeting 139,815 individuals, with 111,071 condemned to death without trial. This operation was part of broader national operations affecting multiple ethnic groups, including Germans, Latvians, Finns, Estonians, Romanians, Greeks, and others.

Polish Operation of the NKVD 11.08.1937

The NKVD's Polish Operation resulted in the sentencing of 139,835 people and the execution of 111,091 Poles, targeting Poles and others perceived as disloyal, leading to family deportations and the annihilation of the Polish minority.

Deportation of Koreans in the Soviet Union 21.09.1937

In 1937, 172,000 Soviet Koreans were forcibly transferred 6,400 km to Central Asia, with up to 50,000 dying due to harsh conditions.

Stalinist repressions in Mongolia 10.1937

Stalinist repressions in Mongolia, between 1937 and 1939, led to the execution of between 20,000 to 35,000 people.

1937 mass execution of Belarusians 29.10.1937

In October 1937, more than 100 Belarusian intellectuals were executed by Soviet authorities. 103 Belarusians killed, with over 100,000 falling victim to broader repressions between 1937 and 1938.

Latvian Operation of the NKVD 03.12.1937

During Stalin's Great Purge, the NKVD arrested and executed thousands of Latvians, with 16,573 out of 22,369 convicted Latvians shot, and cultural institutions were dismantled.

Estonian Operation of the NKVD 14.12.1937

During the Great Purge (1937-1938), the NKVD conducted the Estonian Operation, sentencing 5,680 individuals to various punishments, with 4,672 receiving death sentences.

Finnish Operation of the NKVD 14.12.1937

The Finnish Operation of the NKVD arrested, deported and executed Soviet Finns during the Great Purge of 1937-1938. Finns were 3% of the Karelian ASSR's population but more than 40% of its Great Terror victims; of nearly 6,200 Finnish citizens convicted, more than 4,700 were executed.

Soviet invasion of Poland 17.09.1939

The Soviet invasion of Poland on 17 September 1939 led to the capture of 320,000 Poles and initiated a campaign of mass persecution. Between 1939 and 1941, the Soviet NKVD deported hundreds of thousands of people from eastern Poland.

Katyn massacre 03.04.1940

In 1940, the Soviet secret police executed nearly 22,000 Polish military officers and intelligentsia in Katyn.

Soviet deportations from Bessarabia and Northern Bukovina 13.06.1941

Between 1940 and 1951, the Soviet Union deported 46,000 people from the Moldavian Soviet Socialist Republic.

Soviet deportations from Latvia 14.06.1941

The Soviet government deported approximately 60,000 Latvians to remote and inhospitable regions of the Soviet Union.

Soviet deportations from Lithuania 14.06.1941

Between 1941 and 1952, the Soviet Union forcibly deported at least 130,000 Lithuanians, 70% of whom were women and children, to labor camps. Of these deportees, approximately 28,000 died in exile due to harsh living conditions.

NKVD prisoner massacres in Lviv 22.06.1941

Between 3,500 and 7,000 prisoners in Lviv, including Ukrainians, Poles, and Jews, were brutally executed by Soviet NKVD officers in June 1941, as the Soviets retreated from advancing German forces.

NKVD prisoner massacre in Lutsk 23.06.1941

On June 23, 1941, as the German forces advanced during World War II, the Soviet NKVD executed about 2,000 political prisoners in Lutsk.

Anglo-Soviet invasion of Iran 25.08.1941

The 1941 Anglo-Soviet invasion of Iran led to over 200 civilian casualties in bombing raids.

Deportation of Soviet Germans (1941–1942) 28.08.1941

During WWII, the Soviet government deported at least 846,340 Germans from the Volga region and other parts of the western Soviet Union to Siberia and Kazakhstan due to fears of collaboration with Nazi Germany, causing 228,800 fatalities.

Deportation of the Soviet Greeks 1942

Stalin's regime forcibly uprooted around 70,000 to 80,000 Greeks from the Black Sea coast between 1942 and 1949, with at least 15,000 dying as a result.

Deportation of the Karachays 02.11.1943

The Soviet government deported nearly 70,000 Karachays in November 1943, resulting in at least 13,000 deaths. Despite 20,000 Karachays serving in the Red Army, the government falsely accused them of collaborating with the German forces, leading to their forced relocation to labor camps in Central Asia.

Deportation of the Kalmyks 28.12.1943

Over 93,000 Kalmyks were forcibly relocated to Siberia in December 1943 under false accusations of Axis collaboration, leading to more than 16,000 deaths, despite 23,000 Kalmyks serving in the Red Army.

Operation Lentil (Caucasus) 23.02.1944

In 1944, 496,000 Chechens and Ingush were forcibly deported by the Soviet government, with 123,000-200,000 dying during the transit due to harsh conditions.

Deportation of the Balkars 08.03.1944

In 1944, 37,713 Balkars were forcibly deported from their homeland in 1 day, with 7,600-11,000 deaths, or 20-25% of their ethnic group, as a result of the Soviet government's actions.

Deportation of the Crimean Tatars 18.05.1944

In 1944, at least 191,044 Crimean Tatars were deported by Soviet authorities, resulting in the deaths of nearly 8,000 during transit and tens of thousands more due to harsh living conditions.

Deportation of the Meskhetian Turks 15.11.1944

Between 92,307 and 94,955 Meskhetian Turks were forcibly deported to Central Asia in 1944, resulting in up to 50,000 deaths. The policy was part of a larger Soviet plan to remove Muslim minorities from the Caucasus, leaving the Meskhetian Turks stateless and scattered across 7 countries.

Soviet deportations from Estonia 1945

Between 1945 and 1953, over 35,000 people, mostly women and children, were forcibly deported from Estonia to remote areas of the Soviet Union, constituting a crime against humanity as declared by the European Court of Human Rights. Over 70% of those deported in 1949 were women and children under 16.

Soviet annexation of Transcarpathia 29.06.1945

The Soviet Union annexed Transcarpathia, causing the emigration of over 120,000 people and displacing about 450,000 inhabitants from Czechoslovakia.

Augustów roundup 12.07.1945

The Augustów roundup, conducted by Soviet forces in 1945, led to the arrest of over 2,000 Polish anti-communists, with 600 people executed.

Soviet invasion of South Sakhalin 11.08.1945

The Soviet invasion of South Sakhalin resulted in approximately 5,000 to 6,000 Japanese military and civilian casualties and the capture of around 18,202 soldiers, who were sent to labor camps in Siberia. Additionally, at least 100,000 Japanese civilians fled to avoid Soviet control.

Gegenmiao massacre 14.08.1945

During the Soviet invasion of Manchuria in August 1945, the Red Army massacred over 1,000 Japanese women and children in Gegenmiao, shooting them, running them over with tanks, and bayoneting them after they raised a white flag.

Operation Vistula 28.04.1947

Operation Vistula involved the forced resettlement of nearly 150,000 Ukrainians and others from southeastern Poland to the north and west in 1947, with Soviet approval, to eliminate support for the Ukrainian Insurgent Army. Over 126,000 Ukrainians were apprehended by the NKVD between 1945 and 1947, and nearly 32,000 were killed.

Operation Priboi 25.03.1949

Operation Priboi involved the deportation of over 90,000 people from the Baltic states to Siberia in 1949, primarily women and children under 16, to facilitate collectivization and eliminate resistance. By 1950, more than 4,000 of deportees, including 2,080 children, had died.

Operation North 01.04.1951

Operation North, authorized by the USSR Ministry of State Security, led to the forced deportation of 9,973 Jehovah's Witnesses and their families to Siberia in 1951, where they were classified as "special settlers" and faced severe persecution.

Operation Osen 02.10.1951

Operation Osen in 1951 involved the deportation of over 20,000 people from Lithuania to remote Soviet regions.

Night of the Murdered Poets 12.08.1952

On August 12, 1952, 13 Soviet Jews, including Yiddish writers from the Jewish Anti-Fascist Committee, were executed in Moscow under false charges of espionage and treason.

East German uprising of 1953 17.06.1953

The 1953 East German uprising involved over 1,000,000 people across 700 localities and led to the deaths of over 125 civilians after Soviet forces suppressed the protests.

1956 Georgian demonstrations 04.03.1956

Soviet Army killed at least 21 people and injured 55 in response to 1956 Georgian demonstrations.

Hungarian Revolution of 1956 23.10.1956

The Hungarian Revolution of 1956, lasting 15 days, was crushed by Soviet forces on November 7, 1956, resulting in 2,500 Hungarian deaths and 700 Soviet deaths. The uprising led to the imprisonment of 22,000 people and the execution of 229 individuals, with nearly a quarter of a million Hungarians fleeing the country.

USSR anti-religious campaign (1958–1964) 1958

During Khrushchev's anti-religious campaign (1958-1964), over 50,000 clergy were executed, and the number of functioning Orthodox churches was reduced from over 20,000 to 6,850 by 1972.

Novocherkassk massacre 02.06.1962

The Novocherkassk massacre, where Soviet troops fired on unarmed demonstrators, resulted in 24 deaths and 87 severe injuries.

Soviet–Afghan War 24.12.1979

The Soviet-Afghan War, from 1979 to 1989, resulted in the deaths of between 1,000,000 and 3,000,000 Afghans and displaced 5,500,000 refugees. Soviet forces committed numerous atrocities, including targeting civilians and destroying infrastructure.

Rauzdi massacre 30.06.1983

On June 30, 1983, the Soviet Army killed 24 people in Rauzdi, Afghanistan, including 23 civilians, in retaliation for a confrontation with an 18-year-old anti-communist resistance member.

Kulchabat, Bala Karz and Mushkizi massacre 12.10.1983

The Soviet Army killed up to 360 civilians, including 20 girls and over a dozen elderly, in 3 Afghan villages as a retaliatory measure for the destruction of Soviet military vehicles. Eyewitnesses reported brutal killings, including grenades thrown into rooms filled with women and children.

Kunduz massacre 22.12.1984

During the Kunduz massacre, the Soviet Army killed around 250 civilians in retaliation for Mujahideen resistance. The atrocities included looting, destruction, rape, and the systematic execution of villagers.

Laghman massacre 04.1985

The Laghman massacre involved Soviet forces killing between 500 and 1,000 civilians, employing napalm bombs.

Black January 20.01.1990

In January 1990, Soviet troops entered Baku to suppress anti-Soviet and anti-Armenian sentiments, resulting in 147 civilian deaths and 800 injuries.
